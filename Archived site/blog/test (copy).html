From Pearl
"I started Qube Catalyst because I kept watching the same thing happen. Good founders. Real investment. Products that failed not because the technology was broken, but because nobody had told them the truth."

I’ve spent years watching technology get built in a bubble: founders too close to see what users actually experience, delivery teams defending their decisions, agencies telling clients what they want to hear. Everyone has a stake in the outcome except the person using the thing.

That is the gap I sit in. I have no stake in what your product is, how it was built, or who built it. My only stake is in what is true: what is really happening between your product and the people it is meant to serve, what is quietly breaking trust, and what is costing you users, traction, and money before anyone has named it.

My read on that gap is not theoretical. It comes from 22 years building and shipping technology, including 9 years at Meta London on products used by billions of people, working across engineering, product, and delivery under real pressure, where what holds and what breaks shows up fast.

It also comes from what I have chosen to build and stand for outside the job: founding Black Women in Tech, now a 52,000‑strong community; representing technology companies in policy discussions with government and regulators through ACT; serving as an elected councillor; and living with rheumatoid arthritis. All of it sharpened the same instinct: when something is not right, you do not look away. You go towards it.

Qube Catalyst exists to close that gap and give founders and technology leaders an independent voice with no stake in the outcome except the right one.

Pearl Oguchi
Founder, Qube Catalyst

Services

Three ways to work together

PEARL Readiness Review
£1,999

A whole-product diagnosis. In one focused review, you get a clear, independent view of where your product is breaking — and what to fix first.

Most teams are too close to their own product to see it clearly. Founders, developers, and agency partners all carry a stake in the answer — which makes it hard to see where the product is underperforming, where trust is being lost, or where users are quietly dropping away. This review creates clarity fast, before more effort gets poured into the wrong area.

What's included
  • 30-minute prep call with founder, CTO, or product lead
  • Access to the live or staging product environment
  • Hands-on PEARL diagnostic across all five framework dimensions
  • Review of available usage data and positioning materials
  • 2–3 page executive note with 10–15 prioritised findings
  • 60-minute debrief with clear next-step priorities
The value
A ranked, defensible view of what is wrong, tied to business consequence — not just a list of observations. The 2–3 days of diagnostic work typically prevents weeks of misdirected engineering time.
2–3 days · Delivered within 1 week
PEARL Focus Sprint
£8,000

Diagnosis turned into action. The top two most critical issues identified, shaped properly, and retested — so the team stops guessing and starts moving.

Teams rarely fail because they saw nothing. They fail because they tried to fix too many things at once, let the loudest opinions drive the backlog, or spent engineering time on the wrong problems first. After a whole-product review, the real challenge is deciding which two issues matter most — then making sure those two are shaped and tested properly before more time and money are spent.

What's included
  • Up to 2 heavy days deep in the product for full PEARL diagnostic
  • 10–15 issues surfaced, then prioritised down to the top 2 critical items
  • Working sessions with founder, CTO, and/or product lead
  • Independent shaping of how the top 2 fixes should show up in the product
  • Review of proposed changes before engineering time is committed
  • Retesting in staging or early live use to verify risk is genuinely reduced
  • Short practical summary of what was chosen, what changed, and what to watch next
The value
Stops the team spreading effort thinly. Gives them independent judgement, sharper prioritisation, and support in shaping the right changes — before engineering time is burned on work that does not solve the real problem.
~8 days over 4 weeks · First £4,000 on commencement

The Guarantee

If I find nothing,
you don't pay for it.

This work is only worth paying for if it shows you something real. Something that, left unaddressed, would quietly cost you users, trust, or money. If it doesn't — you shouldn't pay the service fee.

That is not a marketing line. It is how I work. The only way this makes sense as a service is if it consistently finds what it says it will find. And in every test I have run, it has.

PEARL Readiness Review — £1,999
You pay £1,999. I run a full PEARL diagnostic on your product. If, after a complete review, I genuinely cannot surface any meaningful structural gaps — nothing that would quietly hurt users, trust, or traction — I refund the fee in full. No negotiation. No partial charge.
PEARL Focus Sprint — £8,000
You pay the first £4,000 on commencement. I go deep into the product for the first diagnostic phase — typically two heavy days. If, at the end of that phase, I cannot find any meaningful product gaps worth naming, I stop the engagement and return the £4,000 minus a fair charge for those two days of diagnostic time. Once gaps are found and we agree the top two issues, the guarantee has been honoured and we proceed.
PEARL Product Readiness Cycle — £15,000
Same structure. The first instalment covers the diagnostic phase. If the full diagnostic genuinely surfaces no meaningful structural gaps, I return the instalment minus the days already worked. Given the depth of this engagement, the chance of finding nothing is effectively zero. But the guarantee stands because I believe in the work.
What "meaningful structural gap" means
A place where the product quietly breaks trust, blocks activation, leaks retention, or prevents leverage. Not a minor UX annoyance. Something that, left unaddressed, would cost you users, investment, or commercial traction.
