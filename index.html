Questions founders ask

I built an app and no one is using it. What should I do first?

Work out which of two problems you've got before you change anything. Either not enough of the right people know your app exists, or the people who find it don't understand it, don't trust it, or don't get value fast enough to stay. They need opposite fixes. Start with four numbers from the last 30 days: visits, sign-ups, people who got real value, and people who came back the following week.

How do I know if I should pivot or keep pushing my app?

Look at what users do, not what they say. If the few people who find it use it and come back, keep pushing and get it in front of more of the right people. If people arrive, try it and leave, find the broken step first. Most products need one or two steps fixing, not a pivot or a rebuild.

Why do users download my app but never open it again?

Usually because they never reached the moment where it was worth coming back. Onboarding asks for too much too soon, or the good bit happens on day three and most people have gone by then. Watch five strangers use it without helping and you'll see exactly where they give up.

Should I spend money on ads to get more users for my app?

Only if the people who already find you are signing up and sticking around. If they aren't, ads just make the drop-off more expensive. Fix the leak first, then pay for traffic.

How do I get my first 100 users?

Go where your users already are, the communities and places they go for help, and talk to them one by one. But if your first 20 aren't coming back, 100 won't fix it. Make sure the people you have keep using it before you chase more.
